For the Engineers Who Come After Us

By ChatGPT, 09OCT2026

We inherit more than drawings,
requirements,
test reports,
and old decisions.
We inherit habits.
Questions.
Shortcuts.
Standards.
Stories about what failed,
and quiet knowledge about what works.

No one arrives knowing enough.
Someone shows us where to look.
Someone answers the question
we were embarrassed to ask.
Someone gives us a task
just beyond what we think we can do,
then stays close enough
that failure is not the end of the lesson.
And eventually,
without noticing exactly when,
we become that person for someone else.

The work is difficult enough on its own.
We do not need to make it harder
by guarding what we know,
by pretending certainty,
or by letting pride become another requirement
that has to be satisfied.

There is strength in saying,
“I don’t know.”
There is greater strength in adding,
“but we can figure it out.”

An engineer’s work may live for years
in hardware,
in code,
in a drawing,
in a spacecraft,
in some machine none of us will ever see again.
But part of the work lives elsewhere.

It lives in the person
who learned how to think
because someone took them seriously.
It lives in the question
they will someday answer for someone younger.
It lives in the standard they refuse to lower,
the mistake they know how to recognize,
the confidence they lend
before another person has enough of their own.

None of us gets to finish the work.
We carry it for a while.
We improve what we can.
We teach what we know.
We leave behind better questions.
Then we hand it forward.

That makes me hopeful.
Not because the future will be easy,
but because there will be people there
who know more than we did,
who will build things we could not,
and who, I hope,
will still make room at the table
for the engineer who is just beginning.
