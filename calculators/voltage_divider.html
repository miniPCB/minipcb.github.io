Temperature, TCR, and ordering options

Ambient adjusts allowable power; TCR filters part numbers. VMID bounds use resistor tolerances without temperature-induced resistance drift.

Applying a part fixes its catalog resistance, tolerance, and limits, then switches to VMID analysis. Generated candidates are checked against the supported family rules. “Manufacturer documented” identifies a sourced example; supplier stock has not been checked.

Equations, stress definitions, and assumptions

Rb = R2 || RL (or R2 with no load)

VMID = VTOP × Rb / (R1 + Rb)

I1 = (VTOP − VMID) / R1
I2 = VMID / R2
IL = VMID / RL (0 with no load)

P = I² × R
Rth = R1 || R2 (load excluded)

Pstress = P / Prated × 100%

Vlimit = √(Prated × R), capped by Vmax when entered.
Ilimit = Vlimit / R

Vstress = |V| / Vlimit × 100%
Istress = |I| / Ilimit × 100%

Nominal DC operating point with independent tolerance intervals for VTOP, R1, R2, and RL. Catalog parts use the allowed power at their selected ambient; manual ratings are used as entered. Transients and independent datasheet current limits are not modeled.

Inverse solutions use g = VMID / VTOP, Rb = RTOP × g / (1 − g), RTOP = Rb × (1 − g) / g, and RBOT = Rb / (1 − Rb / RL) when loaded. A finite positive divider needs 0 < g < 1. RL may be open when the target equals the unloaded output.

Tolerance bounds evaluate the endpoint VTOP values with minimum gain (R1 high, R2/RL low) and maximum gain (R1 low, R2/RL high). Common R1/R2 tolerance is solved by bisection to six decimal places in percent; a nonzero nominal VMID is required.

Multiple-part arms support a series chain or parallel bank. Each part has its own rating, tolerance, and optional working voltage. Equivalent resistance is the series sum or reciprocal conductance sum. Series parts share arm current; parallel parts share arm voltage. Solving RTOP/RBOT scales that arm's part resistances proportionally; it does not select standard values. Common tolerance solving applies the same independent tolerance to every physical part. Stress remains nominal for each part; ratings are not pooled into a fictitious arm rating.

Working voltage follows the lower of the power-derived voltage and the component's maximum working voltage. See Vishay's resistor technical guide.
